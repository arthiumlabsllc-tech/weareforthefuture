# 1. Purpose of This Brief

This document sets out the full content, information-architecture and functional changes required to reposition the For The Future Organization (FTF) website for its second decade. The objective is not a cosmetic refresh. The website must become the public-facing expression of FTF’s 2026–2031 strategic direction: a structured, credible, safeguarding-conscious, measurable and partner-ready child and youth development organization.

The current website contains strong history, images and individual project pages, but it still largely communicates FTF as a collection of initiatives. The new website should instead explain one coherent development pathway: helping vulnerable children and young people access education, learn, remain in school, live with dignity, build confidence and future-ready skills, receive mentorship, and transition toward productive opportunities.

__Primary website objective  __Make it immediately clear what FTF does, how its work is organised, what impact it has achieved, how supporters can participate, and why institutions can trust FTF as an implementation and social-impact partner.

# 2. Strategic Basis for the Website Refresh

The website should be rewritten and reorganised around the following institutional decisions already reflected in FTF’s strategic and programme documents:

- FTF is entering a new phase of institutional growth after ten years of founder-led and volunteer-powered work.
- The organisation’s work is now organised under five programme pillars rather than a loose collection of stand-alone initiatives.
- FTF should position itself as a child and youth development NGO supporting vulnerable children and youth from disadvantage toward learning, dignity, confidence, skills and future opportunity.
- The organisation should communicate stronger governance, safeguarding, monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning systems.
- The website must serve individuals, diaspora supporters, corporates, foundations, NGOs/INGOs, schools, volunteers, communities and potential implementation partners.
- Giving should evolve from a single generic donation request into clear pathways: general giving, programme support, recurring giving, beneficiary support, partnership and campaign support.

# 10. Programme-Specific Website Changes

## 10.1 STEP / Sponsor a Child

Keep STEP as the flagship holistic education programme. Update copy to explain education sponsorship, progress monitoring, tutoring, health/nutrition support, mentorship, enrichment and transition support. Reframe “Sponsor a Child” into a more structured Sponsor a Child 2.0 / Support a Future journey, subject to final public naming. Profiles must be privacy-safe and FTF-administered.

## 10.2 SmartStart / Foundational Learning

Do not allow SmartStart to remain an isolated programme forever. Present it as an existing Saturday academic-support model that informs FTF’s broader foundational learning, literacy and Learning Clubs strategy. The strategic priority is especially relevant for children who are enrolled in school but are not learning at the expected level.

## 10.3 Empower Her, Period → broader Girls’ Dignity & Retention work

Keep Empower Her, Period as a recognised initiative, but make the pillar page bigger than menstrual-product distribution. Add school retention, dignity, confidence, mentorship, protection awareness, parent engagement, STEM/future exposure and other girl-centred pathways as programming develops.

## 10.4 Future Pathways

Replace “Project Future Ready” as the principal public name with “Future Pathways”, while noting the former name where useful for continuity. Explain the full pathway from career exposure and work-readiness through skills training, internships/apprenticeships, enterprise readiness, mentorship, tools/equipment support where funded, and transition tracking.

## 10.5 Click4Change

Update to include digital literacy, Microsoft/Google productivity tools, public speaking, confidence and replicability. Add the 2026 cohort milestone and future expansion model when approved for publication.

## 10.6 ShareAid

Keep as a partnership-led school and community support initiative, but connect each distribution to education access and retention rather than presenting it only as donation delivery. Include school bags, stationery, hygiene items and complementary education/health components where relevant.

## 10.7 FTF Village

Create a dedicated “Vision Project” page. Clearly distinguish what already exists (secured land / project concept / planning) from what is proposed. Show the development rationale, intended facilities, phased plan, funding objective, safeguarding principles and partnership opportunities. Avoid implying that the residential facility is already operational.

## 10.8 FTF Impact Store & Book Club

Rewrite this page to reflect the current model accurately: the Impact Store is a purpose-driven fundraising and literacy platform currently activated through pop-ups and the A&C Mall partnership rather than presented simply as a conventional permanent shop. The Book Club should be a visible sub-section for children, teens and adults who want to read more without buying every book, with activities such as reading buddies, guided discussions, storytelling, literacy activities, author/book conversations, family sessions, reading challenges and other fun learning experiences. Explain that proceeds and participation support FTF’s wider work.

## 10.9 FTF Nigeria / Project Momentum

Create a clear Nigeria section rather than leaving Nigeria references scattered. Explain that FTF Nigeria operates in Ibadan, Oyo State, with STEP and Project Momentum. Link programmes to the same five-pillar framework so the organisation appears unified across countries.

## 12.1 Ethical Beneficiary Support Rules

- Never show a minor’s full legal name, home address, personal contact information or identifying documents.
- Use positive, current and consented images; no humiliating, distressed or pity-driven imagery.
- Explain the verified need and what support will pay for.
- Supporters fund an FTF-administered need; they do not enter a private financial relationship with a child or family.
- Updates come through FTF and remain subject to safeguarding and consent.
- No uncontrolled direct messaging between supporters and minors.
- Once a need is funded, close it or follow a clearly disclosed reallocation rule.

# 16. Design, User Experience and Functional Requirements

- Mobile-first design with fast load times and compressed, high-quality images.
- Clear visual system for the five pillars, while keeping one unified FTF brand.
- Persistent Give button and clear Partner/Volunteer routes.
- Reusable CMS templates for pillar pages, programme pages, stories, campaigns, reports and team profiles.
- Programme records must support multiple pillar tags.
- One central impact-metrics dataset used across the site.
- Search and filter for News/Stories by pillar, country and content type.
- Forms routed by purpose: general, partnership, volunteer, mentor, school/community enquiry and media.
- Payment system capable of general, programme, campaign and approved beneficiary-need designations.
- Recurring giving and downloadable/emailed receipts where supported.
- Accessibility basics: alt text, keyboard-friendly navigation, sufficient contrast, readable typography and descriptive links.
- Consent-controlled media library or documented process so only approved images are published.
- Analytics and conversion tracking for Give, Volunteer, Partner and key programme CTAs.
- Simple redirect strategy so existing programme URLs do not break when navigation changes.

# 17. SEO and Search Visibility

Each page should have a unique title, meta description, clear H1, structured headings, meaningful image alt text and descriptive URLs. Programme copy should naturally include terms relevant to FTF’s work in Ghana and Nigeria without keyword stuffing.

Priority search themes include: child education NGO Ghana; youth development Ghana; girls’ education Ghana; digital skills for children Ghana; youth employability Ghana; sponsor education Ghana; NGO partnership Ghana; CSR education Ghana; child and youth development organisation Ghana; volunteering with children Ghana; and related Nigeria programme terms where applicable.
